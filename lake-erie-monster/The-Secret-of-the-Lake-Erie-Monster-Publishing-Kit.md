## 18. Names, places, and the newspaper

Cedar Point National Wildlife Refuge is a real refuge in Lucas County, Ohio, managed with Ottawa National Wildlife Refuge. The story uses it. Public fishing access there is limited, and the story is set in early April on a dike. Treat the walk, the hour, and the carcass as fiction even though the refuge is real. The Great Black Swamp, the stone shore from Toledo to Cleveland, and the Erie Islands are the regional frame he writes in his own voice.

The Blade is a real Toledo newspaper. The article in Chapter I is his, including the caption "Lake Erie Monster found—deceased." The jacket must not attribute that report to the paper.

Inside the story, the boys read a legend book by Nehemiah Snippet. They misspell Loch Ness as Lock Ness. One wording has "talked about about" and "much bigger that." The two pastes of Chapter I also differ on small words, including "intact" and "in tact." Pick one paste before typesetting.

Ranger Rick is the name he gives the ranger. It is the same name as a well-known nature magazine. Ask him whether the character keeps it. Mr. Rotert and Jeremy Wolfe are his names. Jeremy Wolfe is the person the made-up article credits with the morning find, two weeks after the boys' first sighting.

Chapter VIII's 256 years comes from a textbook scale the ranger says he is stretching. Leave it as dialogue.

The boys mention *The Lord of the Rings* and a kraken at the gate of Moria. The jacket does not need that joke, and it should not add dialogue from the film or the book.
