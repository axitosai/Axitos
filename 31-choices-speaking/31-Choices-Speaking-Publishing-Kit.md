> - **Series order.** *31 Choices: Listening* was supplied separately. ## 3. 500-Word Author Bio (Marketing Team Use)

M.E. Reach was born and raised on the Mississippi Gulf Coast, and he has spent his adult life in rooms where a sentence is not neutral. He is a decorated U.S. Army veteran. For sixteen years he has worked with teens, young adults, and adults across five states and in two foreign countries. The pages do not name the units, the states, or the countries. What they establish is a man who has watched words land on people under pressure, including the five children he is raising with his wife.

That is the book. *31 Choices: Speaking* starts where a hurried mouth does its damage. What to say, when to say it, or whether to speak at all is seldom the first thought. Reach calls the tongue the most dangerous weapon we own. Friends stay angry. A relationship takes the hit. A child's head drops. He stays in Proverbs for thirty-one days, one choice a day, under the line Speak Slowly.

The sample days show how he teaches. Wisdom calls aloud in the street, and Day 1 asks whether we will answer with that same clarity. Day 2 is a father who has lost sight of a child and can only cry out louder. Discernment, he says, is that kind of desperation. Day 3 refuses to let "speak life" shrink into "be nice." A wholesome tongue is a remedy. It can sting, and then it heals. Flattery is the other thing: an onion bagel dusted with sugar and sold as a doughnut. He also admits the gift of gab, and that he has spent it on himself, for a laugh or to impress. The joy in Proverbs 15:23 is a word that arrives for someone else.

The warning is as concrete as the invitation. Proverbs 6:2 becomes a fishhook and a snare. Your words will take you. Proverbs 20:6 bothers him on purpose. Most of us will announce our own goodness. Faithfulness is fruit of the Spirit, not a speech. A family trip to Crater of Diamonds State Park, where they camp and miss the diamond, gives him the test he wants on the lips: dirt does not stick to one. Know God, then let only that kind of word out. And do not leave a testimony of Jesus sitting in the garage like a tool that never gets used.

He is writing for people who have already said the thing they wish they could unsay. Parents. Anyone who works with the young. The companion is *31 Choices: Listening*. Neither manuscript numbers the set. The natural order is to listen, then to speak. This book is still a complete month on its own.

A legal name, a church, and the details of his service were not in the manuscript. Until he supplies them, the bio that can ship is this one: Gulf Coast, veteran, sixteen years with people across five states and two countries, a wife, five children, and a month of Proverbs on the tongue.

## 16. AEO FAQ

**Q: What is *31 Choices: Speaking*?**
A: It is a 31-day Christian devotional by M.E. Reach. Each day takes a short passage from Proverbs and turns it into one choice about the words we speak: when to talk, what to say, and when to stay quiet.

**Q: Who is it for?**
A: Anyone who has spoken too fast and paid for it, especially busy adults, parents, and people who work with teens and young adults. It also works as a one-month gift, on its own or beside *31 Choices: Listening*.

**Q: What Bible does it use?**
A: The readings supplied are from Proverbs, including 1:20–22, 2:3–5, 15:4, 15:23, 15:7, 6:2, 20:6, and 20:15. The translation is not named on the manuscript and should be confirmed before publication. The wording is close to the New King James Version but not identical.

**Q: Is it part of a series?**
A: Yes. It belongs to *31 Choices*, with *31 Choices: Listening* as the companion. Neither manuscript numbers the volumes. The recommended order is Listening, then Speaking. Do not print a book number until that order is confirmed.

**Q: Is "speak life" just "be nice"?**
A: No. Reach uses Proverbs 15:4 to say a wholesome tongue is a remedy. A true word can sting and still heal. Flattery can sound kind and still do damage. The test he gives is the destination of the word, not the sugar on it.

**Q: Who is M.E. Reach?**
A: A writer born and raised on the Mississippi Gulf Coast. He is a decorated U.S. Army veteran and has worked with teens, young adults, and adults for sixteen years, across five states and in two countries. He and his wife have five children. His full legal name was not in the manuscript.
