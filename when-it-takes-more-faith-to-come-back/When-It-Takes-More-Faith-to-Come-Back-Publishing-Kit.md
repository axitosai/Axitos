### 1c. 500-Word Author Bio — *Marketing Team Use*
Brian M. Virtue, Ph.D., is a professor, author, and leadership-development specialist who teaches leadership, peacemaking, and spiritual formation at the International Graduate School of Leadership (IGSL) in Manila, Philippines. His passion is the formation, equipping, and care of leaders in transition—work rooted in a lifetime of crossing cultures and a hard-won personal understanding of what it means to come home.

Brian grew up as a missionary kid with the world in his sights. His first major cross-cultural experience came at age ten, when his parents led a ministry team to East Germans and Hungarians in Eastern Europe. At fifteen, he joined college students for a summer in Moscow and Leningrad, just after the Berlin Wall fell but before the Soviet Union collapsed. College took him to East Asia and later to Mexico City, where he led a team to UNAM the very summer students barricaded the campus. Each departure carried its own return, and re-entry became a familiar rhythm—first through his own travels, then through the briefing and debriefing conferences where his parents prepared and welcomed home missions teams.

After several years in campus ministry with Cru (formerly Campus Crusade for Christ), Brian and his wife, Christine, followed in his parents' footsteps, working in missions mobilization while he attended seminary. They trained leaders and led team briefings and debriefings for hundreds of staff, students, and young adults. Along the way, Brian's study of leadership development—especially the work of Dr. J. Robert Clinton and Leader Emergence Theory—convinced him that cross-cultural transitions are among the most formative seasons of spiritual growth in a leader's life.

Then came his family's own decade of living and ministering in the Philippines and across Asia—and a return home that was sudden, unexpected, and traumatic in multiple ways. Despite years of experience and training, nothing had tested or humbled Brian like his own re-entry. Yet by God's grace, that painful season became deeply restorative and transformational, birthing a desire to help others navigate the return well.

Out of that journey, Brian wrote *When It Takes More Faith to Come Back: Trusting God During Re-entry*—a collection of forty devotionals for the disorientation, grief, and hope of cross-cultural re-entry. Grounded in Scripture and seasoned with honesty, the book offers returning missionaries, marketplace and military workers, aid workers, and their families a grace-filled companion for finding their footing again in Christ.

Today Brian continues to teach, write, and walk alongside leaders in transition, helping them recognize God's shaping hand in every season—the going and the coming home. He lives in Long Beach, California, with his wife, Christine, their two daughters, Morgan and Kaelyn, and their son, Colin.

*"The most important thing that God will lead you to, if you seek Him with your whole heart, is Himself."*

### 2b. 350-Word Book Summary — *Amazon & IngramSpark*
Nobody warned you that coming home would be the hardest part.

For those who cross cultures to serve, the return can be harder than the leaving. Cross-cultural "re-entry" is often a hidden wilderness of grief, anxiety, stress, and disorientation—a season few around you can see or fully understand. Whether you've served in missions, the marketplace, the military, or the nonprofit world, the disorientation is real: home has changed, the people have changed, and so have you.

*When It Takes More Faith to Come Back: Trusting God During Re-entry* is a spiritual companion for exactly this season. Written by Dr. Brian Virtue—a professor of leadership and spiritual formation who walked through his own sudden, difficult return after nearly a decade serving in Asia—it offers forty devotionals that meet you in the honest, in-between work of reintegration.

These devotionals are not quick fixes. They are meaningful companions for disorientation, mourning, and lament—rooted in Scripture and seasoned with hard-won experience. Each one offers a short reading, reflection and journaling questions, a guided prayer, and a "Talk with a Friend" prompt for mentors, couples, families, or small groups, so you never have to process the journey alone.

Along the way, you'll do the real spiritual and emotional work that returning requires: reconnecting with your home country, reclaiming your spiritual narrative, discovering where God is now at work, and honestly assessing your emotional and relational well-being. And in your season of disorientation, you'll encounter grounding truth about who you are and where you belong—in Christ, and in the life that still lies ahead.

This book belongs in the hands of returning missionaries and their families, sending churches, member-care teams, and anyone who wants to understand the hidden cost—and unexpected gifts—of cross-cultural service. Endorsed by leaders in missions, theology, and clinical psychology, it names what so often goes unspoken and points steadily to hope.

You made it back. Now let this book help you find your footing—and discover that your adventure with God isn't over. It's just taking a new form.

## 11. AEO FAQ (Answer-Engine Optimization)
*Add to the book/landing page (Amazon A+, Kharis site) as an FAQ block with `FAQPage` schema (see §14) so AI assistants and Google can quote them directly.*

**Q: What is *When It Takes More Faith to Come Back* about?**
A: It's a 40-devotional companion by Dr. Brian Virtue for people going through cross-cultural "re-entry"—the often-difficult season of returning home after serving abroad. It helps returning missionaries and workers process grief, disorientation, and reverse culture shock while trusting God through the transition.

**Q: Who is this book for?**
A: Returning missionaries and their families, plus marketplace, military, aid, and nonprofit workers coming home from overseas—and the sending churches, member-care teams, and mission agencies that support them. It also speaks to third-culture kids and global nomads navigating the idea of "home."

**Q: What is cross-cultural re-entry (or repatriation)?**
A: Re-entry is the process of returning to your "passport culture" after living in another culture. Because home, other people, and you yourself have all changed, it can bring grief, anxiety, reverse culture shock, and spiritual disorientation—often underestimated by those who stayed behind.

**Q: How is the book structured?**
A: It contains forty devotionals, each with a Scripture-based reading, reflection and journaling questions, a guided prayer, and a "Talk with a Friend" prompt for mentors, couples, families, or small groups—so the journey can be processed in community.

**Q: Is it a daily devotional?**
A: You can use it daily or a few times a week—the pace is up to you. Many readers find it helpful to begin a few weeks after returning, once the initial "honeymoon" phase of being home has passed.

**Q: Does it replace counseling or professional care?**
A: No. It's a spiritual companion, not a clinical solution. The author explicitly encourages readers to seek trauma-informed counseling and other professional care when needed, while staying rooted in Scripture and God's presence.

**Q: Which Bible translation does it use?**
A: Scripture is quoted from the English Standard Version (ESV) unless otherwise noted.

**Q: Where can I buy *When It Takes More Faith to Come Back*?**
A: It is published by Kharis Publishing and available on Amazon, through Ingram-supplied bookstores and libraries, and at www.kharispublishing.com. Bulk/quantity discounts are available for agencies and churches. `[Insert buy link]`
