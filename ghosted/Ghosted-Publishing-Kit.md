## 3. 500-Word Author Bio (Marketing Team Use)

Edet B. Effiom is a rare kind of voice: someone who has lived on both sides of the story he tells. For more than two decades he has served in pastoral care at Vineyard Church and Ignite Church in Brussels — the crossroads of cosmopolitan Europe — walking alongside people from around the world as they wrestled with doubt, disillusionment, burnout, and the slow ache of a faith that promised life but delivered only routine. He has seen, up close, the very "ghosted generation" his book addresses: leaders running on fumes, congregations gone quiet, and sincere believers who slipped out the back door not in anger but in grief.

That pastoral tenderness is matched by an unusual professional range. Effiom spent years in corporate leadership, including as a Senior Clients Executive serving major projects for Nokia, HP, and Mobistar across French, UK, and Irish markets, and later as a complaints and client-advocacy consultant to Belgacom. Since 2014 he has served as Secretary-General of the Institute for African Empowerment in Brussels, leading cross-cultural teams and advocacy partnerships. He is also a proven innovator — holder of a patent for a metal-cleaning product made entirely from food-grade materials and a silver medalist at the 45th Brussels International Inventors' and Innovators' Exposition (Brussels Eureka '99). His training spans project management at the Université Libre de Bruxelles, communication studies, and degrees in computer science and electronics engineering.

The result is a writer who can hold two things at once: the rigor to diagnose a problem clearly, and the humility to do it without contempt. Effiom is frank that he writes from inside the church, not above it. "I am a product of the very church I am writing about," he says — formed in its Sunday schools, discipled by its pastors, and, by his own confession, sometimes guilty of offering "a manageable, demystified Christianity... strong on explanation but weak on expectation." *Ghosted* is written, in his words, "with ink mixed with tears of both repentance and hope."

His mission is to serve as a gentle but prophetic bridge: calling the Western church back from spiritual amnesia to a living relationship with the Holy Spirit as divine Companion. He writes for three readers at once — the weary pastor, the disillusioned former believer, and the sincere Christian who has never known what they were missing — offering not accusation but invitation. Fluent in English, French, and Dutch, he is equipped to carry that conversation across North America, Europe, and Francophone Africa.

His aim is simple and large: to help catalyze a "Great Return" — not to nostalgia or programs, but to the presence of the Holy Spirit who makes the church a home worth coming back to. *Ghosted: When the Church Forgot the Holy Spirit and a Generation Moved On* is his first book.

*(approx. 500 words)*

## 5. 350-Word Book Summary (Amazon / IngramSpark)

**The church didn't lose a generation overnight. It ghosted the Holy Spirit — slowly, unintentionally — and eventually the silence answered back.**

Across the West, millions have walked away from church. They cite hypocrisy, politics, and hurt, and those reasons are real. But in *Ghosted: When the Church Forgot the Holy Spirit and a Generation Moved On*, pastor and leader Edet B. Effiom argues that beneath them lies a deeper, unnamed wound: a church that became functionally Spirit-less. For all our correct theology on paper, the Holy Spirit was marginalized, systematized, or replaced with programming. We built churches on strategy, spectacle, and safety — and when a generation found no fire, no voice, and no living presence, they assumed God was absent.

Written by a broken-hearted lover of the church from within it — not a critic with a clipboard — *Ghosted* refuses the false choice between honest critique and genuine hope. **Part One** diagnoses the "ghosting": the unintentional drift from Pentecost fire to predictable religion, the unsung burdens crushing pastors, and the symptoms of a Spirit-depleted church. **Part Two** reintroduces the Holy Spirit as a divine Person, not a force — the breath of creation, the Spirit of Christ, the down payment of redemption. **Part Three** offers a practical, grounded road map for Spirit-filled living: rebirth, empowerment, spiritual gifts for the common good, the fruit of the Spirit, and learning to hear God's voice. **Part Four** casts a vision of a re-enlivened church — a "home worth coming back to" — marked by sacred awe, compassionate community, and mission as natural overflow.

Balanced between charismatic excess and cessationist dryness, and grounded from Genesis to Revelation, the book speaks to the weary pastor, the disillusioned leaver, and the sincere believer who senses something missing. And it doesn't stop at reading: with a six-session small-group curriculum, a week of prayers, discussion ground rules, and guides for hard conversations, *Ghosted* is a ready-made resource for congregations that want to move from lament to life.

The Spirit was never gone. We just stopped looking. This is your invitation to breathe again.

*(approx. 350 words)*

## 16. AEO FAQ

**Q: What is *Ghosted* about?**
A: It's a Christian book by Edet B. Effiom arguing that many people left the church not because they rejected Jesus, but because the church functionally sidelined the Holy Spirit — replacing his presence with programs, ritual, and burnout. It diagnoses that drift and offers a hopeful, biblical path to recover the Spirit and renew the church.

**Q: Is this a critique of the church?**
A: It's an honest diagnosis written by someone who loves the church and admits his own part in the problem. Effiom calls it "a family meeting, not a courtroom" — lament paired with a practical vision for renewal, not blame.

**Q: Who should read it?**
A: Weary pastors and church leaders, Christians who feel their faith has gone dry, people who have left the church but still hope it could be different, seminary students, and small groups — across evangelical, mainline, Pentecostal, and post-evangelical settings.

**Q: How is it different from *Forgotten God* or *The Great Dechurching*?**
A: *Forgotten God* is a personal, devotional call back to the Spirit; *The Great Dechurching* is a data-driven study of who leaves and why. *Ghosted* combines institutional diagnosis with Spirit-centered renewal, speaks *to* the dechurched rather than only about them, and includes a full small-group curriculum.

**Q: How is it different from *Holy Ghosted*?**
A: *Holy Ghosted* (Tiffany Yecke Brooks, 2024) treats religious trauma and spiritual abuse. *Ghosted* argues the root issue is pneumatological — a functional loss of the Holy Spirit's presence — and builds a renewal pathway, not only a trauma framework.

**Q: Can a church or small group use it?**
A: Yes. It includes a six-session study guide, a week of prayers, discussion ground rules, and appendices handling objections — designed as a congregational curriculum, not just a book to read alone.

**Q: Who is Edet B. Effiom?**
A: A pastoral caregiver and leader with 20+ years of ministry in Brussels, a former corporate executive, and an award-winning inventor. He writes from inside the church, in English, French, and Dutch. *Ghosted* is his first book.
