## 3. 500-Word Author Bio (Marketing Team Use)

Dr. Phil Smith didn't set out to become an authority on humility. By his own admission, he "completely stumbled onto" the subject — and it changed the trajectory of his life and ministry.

Phil was born and raised in Southeast Asia, the son of missionaries serving with Wycliffe Bible Translators. That cross-cultural upbringing shaped him profoundly — including, as he candidly tells it, giving him an early and uncomfortable education in the subtle dynamics of perceived "superiority" and "inferiority." After studying at Wheaton College in Illinois and George Fox University in Oregon, he married his high-school sweetheart, Jessica — herself part of a missionary family from Southeast Asia — and completed his Master of Divinity at Trinity Evangelical Divinity School.

The couple then answered a call to Petersburg Bible Church on an island in Southeast Alaska, where Phil pastored for ten years. He was later invited back to the lower 48 to help lead an international pastoral-training organization, and over the next twelve years he served in senior leadership roles — including President/CEO — of several international missions organizations, relocating with his growing family to the Chicago area.

It was during his Doctor of Ministry studies at Trinity Evangelical Divinity School that everything converged. For his doctoral project, Phil traveled to four continents, interviewing dozens of leaders about how to cultivate collaboration across organizations and ethnicities. He expected cultural difference to be the great obstacle. Instead, he found something deeper and more pervasive: the damaging attitudes that flow from feelings of superiority and inferiority — and, uncomfortably, their presence in his own heart. The biblical answer he discovered was Jesus-shaped humility. The more he studied, the more central, pervasive, and breathtaking the theme became. As he puts it, he "became transfixed."

In 2021, spurred by that research, Phil founded **promoteHumility**, a nonprofit dedicated to cultivating biblical humility in leaders, churches, and organizations through speaking, workshops, coaching, and transformational initiatives. What began as one person's conviction has grown into a team and a range of initiatives united by a single, surprising claim: that humility is not a peripheral virtue but the very character of God, expressed supremely in Christ.

*The Supremacy of Humility: Reorient Your Life with the Bible's Most Counterintuitive Theme* is Phil's first book — the foundational resource behind his workshops and the fruit of years of study. Written with a winsome, conversational style and global illustrations, it traces humility across the narrative arc of Scripture, from the Psalms to the Lamb on the throne, in short, readable chapters meant to be savored, prayed over, and returned to.

Phil speaks and coaches widely, with a platform rooted in deep networks across the global missions community, local churches, and Christian higher education. His personal mission statement is simple: "To walk humbly with God and with those God places in my life."

Phil and Jessica live in the western suburbs of Chicago, where they are launching their four children — Trevor, Oliver, Linnea, and Lily — into adulthood.

*(approx. 500 words)*

## 5. 350-Word Book Summary (Amazon / IngramSpark)

**What if the most overlooked theme in the Bible is also its most beautiful?**

In a cultural moment defined by self-promotion, personal branding, and shameless boasting, humility can feel quaint — or worse, like weakness. But in *The Supremacy of Humility*, Dr. Phil Smith makes a bracing, carefully developed case: humility is not a peripheral virtue. It is the very character of God, expressed supremely in Christ — the Lamb on the throne.

This isn't a book about trying harder to be humble. It's about falling in love with the humility of God and letting it reorient your entire life. Drawing on both Scripture and hard-won personal experience — including seasons of grief, disability, and his own confronted pride — Phil traces humility across the sweeping narrative arc of the Bible. From the raw honesty of the Psalms, to Jesus' upside-down kingdom, to the mysteries of the eternal Jerusalem, he shows how one counterintuitive thread runs through the whole story of God.

Organized in five movements, the book first fast-forwards to the Bible's startling climax (the Lamb enthroned), then traces the many facets of humility before God (Part 2) and before one another (Part 3), explores the surprising *power* and effectiveness of humility (Part 4), and finally considers God's own perspective on the humble — the poor, the weak, the welcomed, the astounded (Part 5).

Honest about humility's counterintuitive and sometimes uncomfortable demands, Phil writes with a winsome, conversational voice and global illustrations drawn from a life lived across continents. The chapters are short and readable — but, as the endorsers note, readers will profit most if they slow down enough to take this compelling biblical theology to heart. With real-life stories, probing questions, and practical implications for daily life, relationships, leadership, and ministry, it is a book to be read slowly, meditated upon, and returned to again and again.

If you are serious about Christian character and leadership — or simply weary of a world obsessed with self — this is an invitation to something better. Turn the page and dip your toes into an ocean called humility: full of mystery, beauty, and power.

*(approx. 350 words)*

## 16. AEO FAQ

**Q: What is *The Supremacy of Humility* about?**
A: It's an accessible biblical theology of humility by Dr. Phil Smith. Across short, readable chapters, it traces humility through the narrative arc of the Bible — climaxing in Christ, the Lamb on the throne — and shows how God forms humble character that reshapes our lives, relationships, leadership, and ministries.

**Q: Is this a self-help book about "trying harder" to be humble?**
A: No. As the endorsers note, "This is not a book about trying harder to be humble; it is a book about how God forms humble character." It centers on the humility of God revealed in Christ, not on willpower.

**Q: Who should read it?**
A: Everyday church readers uneasy with a self-obsessed culture; pastors, seminary students, and ministry/organization leaders; Christian high-school and college students; international readers; and even curious non-Christians drawn to Jesus' paradoxical life.

**Q: How is it different from other books on humility?**
A: Many recent books are academic, brief overviews, or approach humility from a single lens. This one uniquely traces humility across the whole Bible while staying devotionally accessible and globally (not US-specifically) applicable.

**Q: Is it good for groups, churches, or workshops?**
A: Yes. The short chapters and thought-provoking questions make it well suited to personal devotion, small groups, ministry teams, and as a companion to the author's humility workshops.

**Q: Why is Jesus described as "the Lamb on the throne"?**
A: In Revelation, "Lamb" is the dominant title for Jesus — used more than any other. The book argues this reveals something central about God's character: humility expressed supremely in Christ, which reorients how we read the whole Bible.

**Q: Who is Dr. Phil Smith?**
A: The founder of promoteHumility and a leader with two decades in pastoral and international missions roles (including President/CEO). He holds M.Div. and D.Min. degrees from Trinity Evangelical Divinity School. *The Supremacy of Humility* is his first book.
