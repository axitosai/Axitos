## 5. 350-Word Book Summary (Amazon / IngramSpark)

October fog sits on the road up to Coburg College, and the leaves have turned. Athanasius Summerfield is a freshman, rehearsing the seven liberal arts on his drive, when the president of the college pulls him out of class. His friend Nicholas Obiri is already waiting. There has been an incident. A priceless work is missing from the rare book room, and someone wants this Lutheran school on the Cumberland Plateau publicly shamed, even pushed off its land.

Nicholas is on the pre-seminary track. The campus above the fictional town of Coburg, Tennessee, carries a castle chapel modeled on the German Coburg where Luther once stayed, and a library that has replaced an older building. A Reformation Oktoberfest is part of the school year. So is the ordinary fear of students who are gifted, devout, and not at all sure of the future.

Young wants the reader to reach the last chapter and see that the clue was already on the page. He also wants young people to watch a group of friends who are brave when they are afraid, chaste, serious about Scripture and church history, and still capable of singing too loud in a Mustang. When the investigation meets a darkness that looks demonic, the students look for a spiritual answer as well as a forensic one. He does not build the mystery on a murder. He builds it on a theft, a college that could lose its home, and the question of how these students will live.

Though Devils all the Castle Should Fill takes its title from A Mighty Fortress Is Our God. Young hopes it will be the first Castle College Mystery, in hardcover, for readers from about age twelve through the adults who still want a clean, demanding puzzle. The town and the college are fiction. The faith on the page is his own Lutheran confession, offered as a story rather than as a textbook for the shelf.
