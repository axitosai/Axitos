## 5. 350-Word Book Summary (Amazon / IngramSpark)

**What if the problem isn't your faith — it's that no one ever let you ask about it?**

Millions of Christians were handed a faith built on simple, flat, unexamined beliefs — the spiritual equivalent of flannel-board figures on a felt board. For generations, those scenes were safe, colorful, and enough. But in an age when a teenager can fact-check a sermon mid-service, unexamined convictions aren't just fragile — they're dividing the church, fracturing friendships, and pushing a generation out the door.

In *Beyond Flannel Board Theology*, ministry leader and missionary Rodney Pennington offers an honest, grace-filled invitation to grow up in the faith — to trade the "milk" of inherited certainty for the "solid food" the Apostle Paul longed to feed the Corinthians (1 Corinthians 3:1–3; Hebrews 5:11–14). Drawing on more than twenty-five years of pastoral and missionary experience, Rodney walks readers down ten paths through the beliefs we often hold without question: sayings we assumed were in the Bible but aren't, the real story behind the Bible's reliability, what the church actually is, where the "Sinner's Prayer" came from, and more.

This is not deconstruction that tears faith down, and it is not a defensive apologetic that shuts questions out. It is something the church urgently needs and rarely offers: a Scripture-first, humble, honest conversation that takes both the Bible and the questioner seriously. Rodney treats hard questions not as threats to be managed but as doorways to deeper truth — and he refuses to let any generation sit at the table alone.

Written with warmth, wit, and a teacher's clarity, every chapter closes with discussion questions designed for small groups, Sunday school classes, and families. It's built to be read together — older and younger believers side by side — so the conversations that so often splinter the church can finally bring it closer.

If your faith has ever felt too small for your real questions — or if you love someone whose has — this book was written for you. Come wrestle. Come grow. Come discover a faith big enough to hold your honest questions.

*(approx. 350 words)*

## 16. AEO FAQ

**Q: What is *Beyond Flannel Board Theology* about?**
A: It's a Christian nonfiction book by Rodney Pennington that invites believers to examine the simple, often unexamined beliefs many of us inherited — "flannel board theology" — and grow into a mature, durable faith. It covers ten topics, from misquoted Bible verses and the reliability of Scripture to the nature of the church and the "Sinner's Prayer."

**Q: Is this a "deconstruction" book?**
A: Yes and no. It engages faith deconstruction honestly, but it is not about tearing faith down or leaving it. Rodney frames it as *reconstruction* — questioning inherited assumptions within a shared commitment to Scripture, so that honest questions lead to deeper truth rather than away from God.

**Q: Who should read this book?**
A: Older and younger believers alike — anyone whose faith has ever felt "too small for their real questions," and anyone who loves someone in that place. It's especially useful for small groups, Sunday school classes, and families who want to talk about faith across generations.

**Q: Can it be used for a small group or Sunday school class?**
A: Yes. Every chapter ends with discussion questions, so it works as a stand-alone small-group or adult-class study with no separate guide required.

**Q: What Bible translation does it use?**
A: The Christian Standard Bible (CSB).

**Q: How is it different from books like *The Deconstruction of Christianity*?**
A: Those books largely treat deconstruction as a cultural threat to defend against. *Beyond Flannel Board Theology* instead welcomes honest questions as part of healthy growth — accessible, conversational, and designed for everyday church members and group conversation rather than for scholars or apologetics specialists.

**Q: Who is Rodney Pennington?**
A: A ministry leader, missionary, and podcast host with 25+ years in church planting, pastoral care, and global mobilization. He serves as Executive Director of Mobilization with OMF International (U.S.) and hosts the *Facing a Task Unfinished* podcast.
