## 3. 500-Word Author Bio (Marketing Team Use)

J Allen Jernigan is a storyteller with a historian's eye and a lifelong fascination with the first century — the turbulent, hope-charged world into which the Christian faith was born. He pursued graduate studies in Church History and Second Temple Judaism, immersing himself in the cultural, religious, and political forces that shaped early Christianity, and he has spoken on these subjects in both formal and informal settings, bringing the ancient world vividly to life through engaging, narrative-driven exploration.

*The Far Country: The Reach of an Empire* is the first volume of his debut fiction series — and it is the natural meeting point of his two great loves: rigorous history and compelling story. Rather than retell the Gospels directly, Jernigan reimagines a beloved biblical narrative (the parable of the prodigal son) as a sweeping, multi-generational saga set against the vast machinery of the Roman Empire. The result is a novel in the tradition of *Ben-Hur* and *The Robe* — written for a new generation of readers drawn to the immersive biblical world of *The Chosen*.

His commitment to authenticity is evident on every page. For this series, Jernigan spent considerable time researching the names of real people who lived in first-century Judea, studying biblical characters, Jewish historical records, the writings of Flavius Josephus, inscriptions, ossuaries, and archaeological discoveries. His four principal protagonists — Matthias, Joseph, Yeshua, and Cornelius — all bear names found in the New Testament, each chosen for historical accuracy and for a thematic significance that unfolds across the series. (His decision to name one fictional protagonist "Yeshua," carefully distinguished from Jesus of Nazareth, is explained in a thoughtful author's note that has become a talking point in its own right.)

Jernigan is candid about the craft and the conscience of historical fiction. He takes deliberate creative liberties with conversations, relationships, and timelines to build a cohesive story, while treating Scripture as the highest authority wherever it speaks. As he puts it, his bridges across the silent spaces of history "should never obscure the foundation upon which they stand." His stated purpose is disarmingly simple: "not to rewrite Scripture, but to create a story that leads readers toward it."

That purpose animates the whole series. Though Jesus of Nazareth is not the central character, his life, teaching, death, and resurrection form the theological foundation on which every storyline rests — a quiet, constant presence shaping the journeys of prodigals, soldiers, and seekers alike. In many ways, the series is about what happens when ordinary people encounter the One who stands at the center of history.

Jernigan writes with a vibrant, cinematic style that reviewers describe as hard to put down — painting scenes with words and carrying readers along the intersecting lives of unforgettable characters. His hope and prayer for every reader is twofold: to enjoy a gripping work of historical fiction, and to come away with a renewed desire to explore the Scriptures for themselves.

*The Far Country* is his debut novel and the opening chapter of a much larger story still to come.

*(approx. 500 words)*

**Rome came to the village not to arrest, but to display power — and one boy watched it take everything.**

So begins *The Far Country: The Reach of an Empire*, the sweeping first volume of a new series that reimagines one of the Bible's most beloved stories against the vast, unforgiving backdrop of the Roman Empire.

As Rome's reach stretches across the known world, a single family is slowly pulled apart by faith, ambition, vengeance, and impossible choices. ## 16. AEO FAQ

**Q: What is *The Far Country* about?**
A: It's a faith-based historical novel by J Allen Jernigan, set in first-century Judea under Roman rule. It reimagines a beloved biblical story (the prodigal son) as a sweeping family saga — following one family scattered by grief, ambition, and vengeance, and the grace that makes reunion possible. It's the first volume in a planned series.

**Q: Is this a retelling of a Bible story?**
A: Yes — loosely. It reimagines the parable of the prodigal son (Luke 15) within a richly researched first-century world, weaving original characters around and near biblical events. The author is clear that his aim is "not to rewrite Scripture, but to create a story that leads readers toward it."

**Q: Is Jesus a character in the book?**
A: Jesus of Nazareth is present but not the central protagonist. His life and message move through the story, and in one early scene a fictional character named Yeshua even meets a young Jesus working as a carpenter — a device the author uses to make clear they are separate individuals.

**Q: Why is one character named "Yeshua" if that's also a name for Jesus?**
A: "Yeshua" was a common first-century Jewish name (the Hebrew/Aramaic form behind the Greek "Iēsous" and English "Jesus"). The author gives a fictional protagonist that historically authentic name while always calling the Messiah "Jesus of Nazareth," so readers never confuse the two.

**Q: Who will enjoy this book?**
A: Readers of Christian and biblical fiction, fans of *The Chosen*, and anyone who loves immersive historical novels like *Ben-Hur* and *The Robe*.

**Q: Is it part of a series?**
A: Yes. *The Far Country: The Reach of an Empire* is the first volume; the names and storylines are designed to carry significance across subsequent books.

**Q: Who is J Allen Jernigan?**
A: A writer with a lifelong passion for the first century who pursued graduate studies in Church History and Second Temple Judaism and brings that research to life in narrative fiction. *The Far Country* is his debut novel.
