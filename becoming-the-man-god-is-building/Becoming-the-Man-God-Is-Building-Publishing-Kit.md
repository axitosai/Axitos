Wedzer Chery is a faith-driven author, a licensed claims adjuster, and an entrepreneur in Boynton Beach, Florida. Becoming the Man God Is Building is his first book. ## 5. 350-Word Book Summary (Amazon / IngramSpark)

Becoming the Man God Is Building is a Christian book for men who are tired of moving and still not changing. Wedzer Chery begins where many men actually live: working, paying what is due, chasing the next goal, and still waking up with the sense that the life in front of them is thinner than it should be. The opening is not a dramatic altar call. It is the moment a man admits that success on the outside has not answered who he is becoming on the inside.

From there the book treats calling as something a man can recognize and still resist. Purpose rarely arrives with a full map. It arrives as a conviction, then as a choice to move before every step is visible. Chery is plain about the next danger. A man can hear that call and run the other way. The later chapters ask him to face himself, break old patterns, and accept discipline as the ordinary way growth is built. Failure is not the end of the story. It is one of the teachers. Humility is the door that pride keeps shut.

The middle of the book is about people. The right influences pull a man toward character. The wrong ones make drifting feel normal. Chery then sets character beside faith in seasons that do not explain themselves, and he ties leadership to responsibility rather than to attention. Service, patience, courage, and integrity are not separate speeches. They are the tests that show whether the earlier lessons took. The last movement is purpose, and then the title itself: the man God is building is still in the work.

He wrote a continuous process a man can read straight through, alone or with a group. A men's group, a church ministry, or a local mentor can walk that same path with him week by week. Chery writes as a licensed claims adjuster and an entrepreneur in Boynton Beach, Florida, and this is his first book. He is still in the process he describes. He asks the reader to stay in it, one decision at a time.

*(350 words)*
