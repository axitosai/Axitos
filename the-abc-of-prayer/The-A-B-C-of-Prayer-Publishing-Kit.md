> **Confirm before the record is locked:**
> - **Subtitle.** The title page reads "Understanding Prayer Book by Book." The proposal reads "contextualizing prayer book-by-book." Sell the title-page subtitle. Keep the hyphens in **A-B-C**. Endorsers often write "ABC."
> - **Byline.** Title page and copyright say Tommy Mthembu. The legal name is Wilfred Tommy Mthembu. Endorsers call him Dr. His own bios list a medical degree, a fellowship in ophthalmology, a B.Th., and an M.Div. They do not list a doctoral degree. Keep **Dr.** off the jacket unless he asks for it. Do not add PhD, and do not invent the letters of the medical degree.
> - **Two names for the men's group.** The short profile says Men of Vision and Enlightenment. The long bio says he co-founded MOVE. Do not treat those as one organization in public copy until he confirms it. Do not expand MOVE on your own.
> - **Three parts, not two.** The contents and the author's "how to use" section describe three parts. Professor George N. Malebe's preface says two. Follow the contents. His preface may have been written to an earlier outline.
> - **STAPLER.** Malebe expands it as Supplication, Thanksgiving, Adoration, Praise, Lamentation, Ear, and Repentance. The author's summary names supplication, thanksgiving, adoration, praise, lamentation, and repentance, and then talks about a two-way conversation. Confirm that E is Ear before the diagram is printed.
> - **Bible translation.** The copyright page has no permission line. The Psalm 25 extract ("Show me thy ways, O Jehovah") matches the American Standard Version very closely. Other quotations read more like contemporary English. Name every translation. ASV is public domain. Anything still in copyright needs a line.
> - **Interior.** The foreword's John 16:23–24 quotation is cut off at the start ("ask the Father in My name..."). Chapter 17 is titled "The Prayer of Zachariah." Decide whether that stays or becomes Zechariah. Do not pull the preface's Nostradamus sentence, or its note on the courtroom meaning of "prayer" in South African law, into an advertisement.
> - **Not a series.** *Sermons from the Fount* is his earlier book. One endorser reads this as a continuation of that book's eye imagery. His own account is a prayer manual. A devotional and a journal are a hope he stated, not books under contract. Do not number this volume.
> - **Private contact.** A street address, a mobile number, and a personal email were on the proposal. They stay in the publisher's file. They are not repeated here.
> - **What he reported, not what we verified:** three Facebook accounts with about 1,500 followers combined, a group of about 50 men, access to two unnamed radio stations, two launch venues, a dormant Buzzsprout podcast, and an expectation of 800 to 1,000 author copies. Reconfirm the copy count and the exchange rate before a print order.

## 5. 350-Word Book Summary (Amazon / IngramSpark)

**You already know you should pray. The harder part is knowing what to do when you sit down.**

The A-B-C of Prayer is Tommy Mthembu's answer to that morning. He wrote it after a winter Saturday when his own prayer room felt too hard to enter, and the reading in front of him was Psalm 25, David's confidence in prayer. The book treats prayer as a discipline and as an exercise. It does not rush to a testimony and then look for a verse. It takes the biblical story as the testimony, and it reads each prayer in the setting where it was first spoken.

Three parts carry that work. The first asks what the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit each do when a person prays, from the Lord's Prayer to the promise of praying in Jesus' name to the Spirit who intercedes when we do not know what to say. The second walks the Bible book by book: David, Jesus in Luke and in John, the church in Acts, penitence and intercession, Hezekiah, the prophets, Habakkuk, the Epistles, the prayer of agreement, Zechariah, prayer and song, blessing, and prayer that asks for what only God can do. The third is the practice. How to pray. How to keep a life of prayer in balance. How to finish.

At the center of that practice is the STAPLER Prayer Guide, a simple sequence for a prayer that would otherwise sprawl: supplication, thanksgiving, adoration, praise, lamentation, the ear that listens, and repentance. The appendix is a workbook, and the author says the book closes with an illustrated Prayer Wheel for groups who want to pray all the kinds of prayer, not only the one they already prefer. Most chapters also name a place to begin today.

Mthembu pastors Redemption Bible Church in Soweto, Johannesburg. He is an ophthalmologist, a teacher of Scripture, and the author of Sermons from the Fount. He writes from South Africa for prayer groups, intercessors, worshippers, and any Christian who has stalled. One book. The prayers of Scripture, in their own words. Then yours.

*(348 words)*
