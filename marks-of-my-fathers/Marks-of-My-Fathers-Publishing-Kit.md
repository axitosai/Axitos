## 6. Book Genre & Sub-Genre

**Primary:** Adult memoir. He calls it a story of fatherhood, sexual sin, pride, and the years after. The supplied chapters are narrative, not a sermon and not a study guide.
**Second:** A Christian memoir told from inside the story. Chapter 1 asks what the word father has meant, earthly and divine. He says faith should surface from the scenes, not from a three-step testimony.
**Not this shelf:** A self-help manual for sexual addiction, a youth curriculum, a sermon collection, or an Asian American immigrant memoir. He says the Asian American books he names are a neighboring shelf, and that his setting is Singapore. Do not file this as young adult because he hopes teenagers will read it. The pitch includes sexual lust. Those chapters were not attached.

**What Chapter 1 contains.** A cremation. The casket moves. His mother sobs. Yeye died the same day and was cremated the same day. Barry is twenty-one, just out of mandatory military service, in his first year of university. He had read parables at the bedside and received a smile and a shake of the head. He asks where Yeye's soul is, and why prayer did not bring the answer he wanted. He sets that silence next to Pa's absence: birthdays with an empty seat, promises not kept, his mother carrying the household while Pa worked. Other goodbyes are listed without names: a soccer game, a hospital blanket, a phone call whose "last seen" froze. He asks whether the marks of both fathers can be rewritten.

**What Chapter 2 contains.** A holiday in Penang. A slow cooker falls and burns Robin, a year younger than Barry, who was four. Emma, the helper, is sure she should have been in the room. Mum drives an illegal turn and says she paid the police on the spot. The burn leaves a mark on Robin's thigh. Weekends without Mum. The drive across the Singapore–Johor Bahru Causeway to Yeye and Popo. Pa works in Kuala Lumpur and comes home every few months, switching cars in Johor so he will not pay the road tax. The family had lived in Klang, then moved to Singapore when Barry turned five. Yeye's stories, as Mum tells them: a coin turned into a chilli trade, snacks from the Japanese during the occupation, a night on a boat when a police light found them and then moved on. Pa at home: Beng Thin, beancurd on Rochor Road, a game of lifting Barry onto his feet. Emma, from Alabat Island in the Philippines, hired when Barry was a newborn, still there thirty-three years later, with nine grandchildren. A cane comes out at dinner when Barry will not sit. Emma slams the bowl down. Mum finishes the feeding.

**What the pitch claims and the sample does not show.** Elite schooling and a hunger for affirmation. A cross-cultural relationship and sexual addiction. Digital temptation, shame, and confession. Military despair beyond the fact that he had just finished service at twenty-one. Family secrets after the father's death. Kiasu, named as a competitive fear of falling behind. Singlish, introduced slowly. Do not write those scenes until the pages are in the file.

**Two back covers.** The proposal's own back cover is the one to quote. The later marketing package rewrites it in a humor tone and adds a line telling the reader to call his father. That line is not in the proposal back cover. Do not add it.
