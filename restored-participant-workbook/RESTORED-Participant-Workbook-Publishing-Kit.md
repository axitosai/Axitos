**Primary:** Adult Christian Bible-study workbook for a small group, built as the participant companion to a novel.
**Second:** Christian living and personal application. Lesson 1 moves from scripture to an affirmation, a prayer, and a weekly challenge.

**What Lesson 1 contains.** Theme: Faith, Family, and What We're Built On. The overview says something was given before everything was taken, and that a foundation can remain when a person believes it is gone. Participants underline words in four scriptures and write what stood out. They answer Earl. They copy an affirmation and then rewrite it for their own life: the faith planted in them cannot be uprooted by what they have been through, and they carry it even when they cannot feel it. Before the group they answer at least two of five questions. After the group they record one or two takeaways. The week's challenge is to write three things taught in childhood, about God, about character, and about how to treat people, and to thank God for the people who planted them. Then they write their own prayer, choose one scripture to keep, and use the notes page. The front of the book asks for a name, a group, and a start date, and says nobody else needs to read it.

**The five questions, in short.** What childhood foundations still show up. Who believed in you before you believed in yourself. The protagonist's father expressed love by showing up rather than by words; how has love reached you in a way you missed at the time. What a spiritual foundation is, set against religious exposure. The workbook says the protagonist described his home as built on "faith and love" rather than money, and asks what your home was built on and what you want to build on now. That last claim about the novel was not in the chapters supplied for the novel. Keep it as the workbook's question until the page is found.
