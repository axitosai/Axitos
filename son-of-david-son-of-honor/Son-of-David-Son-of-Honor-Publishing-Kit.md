**What Chapter 1 actually contains.** Timaeus, a Greek metalworker, returns to the port of Piraeus from a month of selling tools in Israel. He will not forge weapons. At home, outside Athens, his son Bartimaeus, age twenty-five, is secretly shaping a dagger. Sister Alexandra finds it. Mother Anastasia welcomes Timaeus. A month later Alexandra marries Leonidas, a farmer of olives and grapes, in a three-day Greek wedding. Father and son give her a silver necklace with an amethyst. Timaeus tells his son that bar means son and Timaeus means honor. The chapter ends on that name. Bartimaeus can see. The blind companion named in the introduction does not appear.

**How he frames the Gospels.** Mark names Bartimaeus. Matthew 20 speaks of two blind men. Luke 18 speaks of a certain blind man. He says this is one event, with Mark choosing to focus on a man who may have been known to Mark's readers. He also says Matthew 9:27–31 is a different miracle, even though the cry "Son of David" is similar. He points to other healings of the blind in Matthew 12:22, Matthew 15:30–31, Mark 8:22–25, and John 9:1–7. He cites Deuteronomy 4:2 and Revelation 22:18–19 and says the fiction is not an attempt to add to Scripture. Record that as his boundary. The harmonizing of Matthew, Mark, and Luke is his reading.

**Scripture notes for production.** No translation is named. "And Jesus stopped" is the opening of Mark 10:49 in the English Standard Version. The New International Version starts "Jesus stopped," without "And." The New King James Version says Jesus stood still. His next sentence, "And Jesus waited," is not in the verse. His mission statement cites Mark 12:30 and writes "serve" where the verse says love. Chapter 1 quotes a proverb with no label. The wording sits near Proverbs 22:1 and matches neither the New American Standard Bible 1995 ("great wealth") nor the New International Version ("to be esteemed") exactly.
