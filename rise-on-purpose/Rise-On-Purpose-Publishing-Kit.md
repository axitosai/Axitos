## 3. 500-Word Author Bio (Marketing Team Use)

Pastor Patrick McKenzie, M.Div., MAASE, writes for people who feel made for more and cannot yet name it. Leaders who serve quietly. Visionaries wrestling with doubt. Believers who wonder if their season has already passed. He is a pastor, mentor, and speaker, and *Rise On Purpose: Discovering God's Plan When Life Tries to Define You* is his invitation to stop surviving and start walking in an assignment.

He is plain about how the book started. For a long time he was not sure he had anything to say that anyone would want to hear. What he did know was the feeling of being called and still stuck: a vision in his heart, and doors that would not open. No, you're not qualified. No, we're going in a different direction. No, your idea doesn't fit here. No, your past disqualifies you. Meetings ended in polite rejection. Prayers felt like they were echoing. His conclusion, and the spine of the book, is that you do not need every door. You need the one God ordained.

That door, he says, opened where he had not been looking, and it did not come through connections, popularity, or striving. It came when he laid down his timeline and his need for validation. What replaced approval was an assignment. The closed doors had not canceled the calling. They had been preparation for the one yes. *Rise On Purpose* is the product of that yes.

He is careful about what the book is not. It is not hype, and it is not a promise that fame equals impact. Rising, in his framing, is not reinvention. It is revelation: living from a calling instead of performing for it. Obedience, he says, is what qualifies you when you do not feel qualified.

The book is built as a path, not a pep talk. Part I, Uncover It, asks readers to find the purpose already in them: born with it, lost in the fog of the familiar, shaped by pain, and taught to listen to the pull. Part II, Build It, treats structure as spiritual: start before you feel ready, steward your stage, and stay hungry and holy at the same time. Part III, Release It, turns toward authority, legacy, the plateau, and the difference between purpose in public and wholeness in private. A final chapter, "The Rise Continues," leaves the work open rather than tied off.

He writes to four readers he names himself: the dreamers, the discouraged, the delayed, and the determined. People with a big vision in a small space. People who prayed for clarity and got fog. People whose passion has thinned into fatigue, and who are still holding a flicker. For each of them the promise of the book is the same one he needed: closed doors do not cancel a calling, and the right open door does not merely let you in. It launches you.

His stated mission is simple: inspire others to rise with integrity, clarity, courage, and unwavering purpose in Christ. *Rise On Purpose* is that mission in book form.

*(about 500 words)*

## 5. 350-Word Book Summary (Amazon / IngramSpark)

**Closed doors do not cancel a calling.**

Pastor Patrick McKenzie knows the sound of no. Not qualified. Wrong direction. The idea doesn't fit. A past that someone else treated as disqualifying. He also knows the one door that opened when he stopped managing the timeline and surrendered it — and how that single yes changed more than his opportunities. It changed what he believed God had been doing in the noes.

*Rise On Purpose: Discovering God's Plan When Life Tries to Define You* is the book that came out of that yes. He is clear about what it is not. It is not a success manual, and it is not hype. It is a soul manual for people who want to rise with integrity, clarity, holiness, and power. Fame is not the goal, and flawlessness is not the requirement.

The path has three movements. **Uncover It** helps you name the purpose already in you — the thing you were born with, the fog of the familiar that hides it, the pain that often meets purpose on the way, and the pull you have to learn to hear. **Build It** treats structure as spiritual: start before you feel ready, steward the stage you actually have, and stay hungry without losing holiness. **Release It** turns outward: authority, a work that outlives you, the plateau that stalls so many callings, and the difference between looking purposeful in public and being whole in private.

McKenzie writes for the dreamers, the discouraged, the delayed, and the determined. For the person with a large vision in a small room. For the one who prayed for clarity and got fog, and for the one whose fire has burned down to a flicker. Rising, as he frames it, is not becoming someone new to impress a crowd. It is revelation: agreeing with an identity God already spoke, and living from the calling instead of performing for it.

If you are in a valley or at the edge of a breakthrough you do not feel ready for, this is the door he wished he had been handed. One door. The one that launches you.

## 15. Target Audience & Reading Level

- **Primary:** Believers who feel made for more and cannot name it — the dreamers, the discouraged, the delayed, and the determined, in his words. People who have been told no often enough to wonder if the calling was real.

- **Group use:** The three-part structure (Uncover, Build, Release) can carry a 12-week group if a discussion guide is added. The manuscript as supplied does not include one. That is a product decision, not a claim that one exists.
- **Reading level:** Direct, pastoral, spoken-word cadence. Adult Christian living. Roughly 8th–10th grade. No seminary background required, including for readers who do not share his M.Div.
- **Tone:** Urgent and encouraging. He distinguishes ignition from mere inspiration, and resolve from hype.

## 19. Comparable Titles (Comps)

He did not supply competitive titles. These are positioning comps only. Confirm the edition and ISBN before they appear on a sell sheet.

1. **The Purpose Driven Life** — Rick Warren | Zondervan, 2002 (later expanded editions)
   *How this book differs:* Warren offers a 40-day life-purpose journey and defined the category. *Rise On Purpose* is narrower and more urgent: identity under rejection, one assigned door, and a three-part path from uncovering a calling to releasing it.    *Why it sits nearby:* Identity and the inner voice, for the same Christian-living reader. McKenzie's book is organized around calling and closed doors rather than around Psalm 23.
