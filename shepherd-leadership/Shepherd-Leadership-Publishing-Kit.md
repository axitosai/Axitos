## 3. 500-Word Author Bio (Marketing Team Use)

Traye Hogge has spent his career doing the kind of work most leadership books never mention. Before he ever led a team, he worked with his hands: in mobile maintenance and facilities management, as a police officer and executive-protection specialist, a locksmith, a cabinet maker, and a low-voltage and CCTV technician. That path led him into HVAC, where he started as a technician, became a Technical Response Analyst, and later a District Manager who rebuilt a service office's culture from the ground up.

Today he is **Senior Manager of Learning Operations for Sales at Daikin Applied**, where he leads the **College of Sales** — overseeing onboarding, training, and development for sales teams across the organization. He holds a degree in Business Management and Organizational Development, an MBA, and is a Certified Facility Management Professional (IFMA). The credentials came the hard way. Passed over more than once by leaders who still saw only a technician, he left clear-eyed rather than bitter — and he refuses to make the people in his own care wait the way he did.

That crossing — from labor to leadership, one unglamorous job at a time — is the thesis of his first book, *Shepherd Leadership: Crossing Over from Labor to Leader*. Traye's premise is simple and demanding: leaders are shepherds. Not in a soft, sentimental sense, but in the practical sense of Psalm 23, which he reads as a job description. A shepherd does not exist for the sheep's admiration. The shepherd exists for the sheep's well-being. Success is no longer what you produce. It is who others become under your care.

The book turns that idea into a field manual of twenty-five short chapters: the identity shift a promotion requires, then a toolkit — the rod of standards, the staff of guidance, the sling of courageous decisions — and the terrain leaders shape, from vision and conflict to the valleys that test them. An appendix of exercises moves the reading into the week ahead.

The conviction underneath is personal. At sixteen, his father took him to his grandfather's grave and said only, "Son, whatever you do, don't mess up your grandfather's name." Traye carries both their names, and he has come to believe leadership begins exactly there: you carry something that was given to you, you are responsible for it, and what you do with it outlasts you. He writes for anyone promoted into leadership without ever being taught how to lead — in the trades, in operations, in sales, in the church, and in every place where results matter and people matter more.

A Christian and longtime resident of Virginia, Traye lives with his wife and family on land where he is slowly building a family compound. When he is not writing or training the next generation of sales leaders, he is flying drones, fabricating custom slingshots, or spending time with his growing family — including a grandson recently named in his honor.

*(approx. 490 words)*

## 5. 350-Word Book Summary (Amazon / IngramSpark)

**The skills that earned you the promotion are not the skills the role requires.**

For years, Traye Hogge's value was measured by output. Fix the problem. Close the deal. Deliver the result. Competence earned trust, and trust earned opportunity. Then he began leading people — and discovered that leadership is not an extension of labor. It is a different discipline entirely. He was still producing. He needed to start shepherding.

*Shepherd Leadership: Crossing Over from Labor to Leader* is a field manual for that crossing. It is for the technician, the operator, the salesperson, the tradesperson, and the ministry leader who has just been handed responsibility for other people and quietly realizes no one ever taught them how. Hogge's premise is blunt: leaders are shepherds. Not in a sentimental sense, but in the practical sense of Psalm 23, which he reads as a job description. A shepherd does not remove the valley. He walks through it with the flock. He does not drive from behind. He walks ahead.

The book moves in four parts. **Crossing Over** names the identity shift a promotion demands — and the telltale signs you haven't made it yet, like still doing the work yourself because it's faster, or feeling threatened when someone on your team outperforms you. **The Shepherd's Toolkit** puts three ancient instruments to modern use: the rod of standards and protection, the staff of guidance and coaching, and the sling of courage and decision. **The Shepherd's Environment** maps the terrain leaders actually shape — vision, empathy, conflict, teamwork, growth, refreshment, and the valley. **The Shepherd's Legacy** looks past your own tenure to strategy, momentum, mentorship, succession, and how to lead after you have failed.

Hogge writes from the jobs most leadership books skip: police work, locksmithing, cabinet making, HVAC service, and, today, the College of Sales at Daikin Applied. He is honest about being passed over, and about the name he carries from his father and grandfather. An appendix of exercises carries the reading into the workweek.

This is not soft leadership. It is disciplined, decisive, and deeply human — and it measures success by who others become.

*(approx. 340 words)*

## 15. Target Audience & Reading Level

- **Primary audience:** Newly promoted leaders — technicians, operators, salespeople, tradespeople, and supervisors — who were excellent at the work and have not yet been taught how to lead people.
- **Secondary audience:** Experienced managers who want a reset, and the learning-and-development, HR, and sales-enablement leaders who onboard them. The author's College of Sales role is a direct channel.
- **Church audience:** Developing church leaders, elders, and ministry staff. Endorsements from pastors and a retired Navy captain who serves as an elder point here. Captain Katz calls it a manual "that needs to be read and understood by developing church leadership."
- **Gift and group use:** A promotion gift, a new-manager cohort read, a church leadership class. Short chapters plus an appendix of exercises suit a weekly group.
- **Reading level:** Plain, concrete, adult trade nonfiction. No seminary or MBA required to read it. Roughly 8th–10th grade. Scripture is NIV and used as a working model, not as academic theology.
- **Tone:** Direct, experienced, unsentimental. Shepherding here means responsibility, not softness.

## 16. AEO FAQ

**Q: What is *Shepherd Leadership* about?**
A: It is a faith-informed leadership book by Traye Hogge for people crossing from doing the work to leading the people who do it. He treats Psalm 23 as a practical job description: protect, guide, walk ahead, and measure success by who others become.

**Q: Who is it for?**
A: New managers and supervisors in trades, operations, sales, and corporate roles, and developing church leaders. It is for anyone promoted into leadership without being taught how to lead.

**Q: Is this only a church book?**
A: No. The model is biblical, and the applications are workplace-first: standards, coaching, hard decisions, conflict, teamwork, and succession. Church leaders are a second, natural audience, not the only one.

**Q: What is the "rod, staff, and sling"?**
A: Hogge's three tools. The rod is protection and standards. The staff is guidance and coaching. The sling is courage and decision-making. Later chapters apply them to vision, conflict, growth, and legacy.

**Q: How is it different from other shepherd-leadership books?**
A: Most aim at pastors. This one is written by someone who came up through the trades, law enforcement, and HVAC service, and who now trains sales leaders. The subject is the promotion itself — the identity shift from laborer to leader.

**Q: Does it include discussion or training material?**
A: Yes. An appendix of exercises and reflection prompts is written to take the chapters into daily practice, and the chapters are short enough for a team or leadership class.

**Q: Who is Traye Hogge?**
A: Senior Manager of Learning Operations for Sales at Daikin Applied, where he leads the College of Sales. His career ran through the trades, police work, and HVAC leadership before corporate learning and development. *Shepherd Leadership* is his first book. He lives in Virginia.
