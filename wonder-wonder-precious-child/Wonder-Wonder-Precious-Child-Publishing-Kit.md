## 5. 350-Word Book Summary (Amazon / IngramSpark)

**One last book, with the lights already low.**

The day is over. The room is quiet. Someone small is still awake, and someone who loves that child has one book left.

Wonder, Wonder, Precious Child is Dan Yuen's bedtime picture book for that moment. It starts from the nursery rhyme almost every parent already has in the mouth, Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star, and it does not stay a song about a star. The rhyme walks a brother and a sister through the wonders of God's creation and keeps asking how such things came to be. Jenny Shute's oil paintings stay with those two children from page to page. The last stanza, as Yuen describes it, sends the listener back out to keep exploring, because the world points to the Creator, and the Creator is working a plan.

This is a read-aloud for children who are not reading yet. He names newborns and toddlers, the parents who buy for them, and the people who bring a gift to a new family. A church nursery and a church library are on his list. He wants the experience of a classic bedtime book, the slow page and the repeated music of a rhyme, with a Christian word that an adult can hear too. The text he estimates is only 200 to 250 words. The paintings have to do as much work as the lines.

Yuen wrote the poem out of children's messages he once gave in church, the few minutes before the children left the service, when he noticed the adults were listening. Shute comes to the pages from years of reading aloud to young children and from two devotionals she has already illustrated. Together they are offering a keepsake, not a lesson plan. Peace is the mood. Wonder is the word he put in the title twice. Read it with the lights low, and leave the last page open a moment longer.

A parent does not have to explain the faith in one night. The rhyme does the introducing. The child supplies the looking. Tomorrow night, the same pages will be there.

*(350 words)*
