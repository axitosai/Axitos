## 5. 350-Word Book Summary (Amazon / IngramSpark)

Many of the women Suzanne Bohman worries about are already in church. They feel left out, ashamed, or tired of pretending they belong. Dear Sisters is her letter to them, written with excerpts from her daughter, Leanna Grace Bohman. It is not a program for private spiritual success. It is an invitation to heal in the open, and then to love the people in the next pew.

The road has four stretches. First, be loved as a daughter of God: prepare for vulnerability, receive his love, and take his hand when fear is loud. Next, walk with Christ and with others in honesty, in the unity of the Spirit, and in humility. Then love God with a cultivated spirit, a turned back from broken cisterns, and a life practiced in his presence. Last, love people: become a sister, learn the strength of a Proverbs 31 woman, use the gifts in hand, and be a blessing. Jesus' two great commands, to love God and to love the neighbor, are the spine.

Suzanne writes as a counselor in West Virginia who has spent more than twenty years with people in pain, and as a woman who has felt invisible in her own church. She uses Hagar, seen by God in the desert, and she uses the hard command to love the folks who are difficult. Leanna writes from a younger place, including a college night when she went to be strong for a friend and found she was the one who needed tea and a hug. A later page in Suzanne's voice sits with a hard medical day and a small crash, and asks where Jesus is when the valley is ordinary and sharp.

The draft is finished, in her report, at about forty-two thousand words. She hopes women's ministries and Bible studies will use it, and she hopes a group guide will follow. That guide is not written yet. What is written is the letter: come be honest, come be loved, and come be a sister on purpose. No publisher name and no ISBN number are printed on the draft yet.

*(350 words)*
